\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\thispagestyle{plain}
\markboth{Abstract}{}

\begingroup
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}

The thesis focuses on addressing the critical issue of railway ballast
degradation, which significantly impacts the safety, stability, and
operational efficiency of railway tracks. Tailored specifically to the unique
conditions of Indian Railways, this research aims to develop a deeper
understanding of ballast behaviour under impact loading and to predict the
quantum of ballast degradation using 3D image processing as well as
machine-learning models.

\bigskip

At the core of the study is the modification of the European-standard
drop-weight impact test apparatus to better align with Indian railway
ballast specifications and operational conditions. The modified setup
accurately replicates on-field stress conditions experienced by ballast in
the Indian context, providing a more relevant and reliable framework for
analysis. The modified apparatus serves as a critical tool for evaluating
ballast degradation under controlled yet realistic conditions, delivering
nominal contact stresses of \SI{275}{\kilo\pascal} (at a
\SI{300}{\milli\metre} drop height) and \SI{523}{\kilo\pascal} (at a
\SI{450}{\milli\metre} drop height)~--- corresponding to the
\SI{22.5}{\tonne} axle load characteristic of Indian Railways.

\bigskip

A significant innovation in this study is the use of advanced 3D image
processing techniques to evaluate morphological changes in ballast particles
before and after impact testing. High-resolution 3D meshes are captured to
analyse changes in particle shape, size, and texture, providing a detailed
and quantitative understanding of ballast degradation. A paired-particle
protocol applied to 45 marked tracer particles across nine experimental
configurations enables precise measurement of deterioration, allowing
identification of critical thresholds and damage modes (\gls{ModeA} --- diffuse
abrasion vs.\ \gls{ModeB} --- catastrophic fracture) that are not detectable
through traditional sieve analysis.

\bigskip

The outcomes of this study contribute to railroad ballast degradation
assessment using image-based morphology and machine learning, delivering a
scalable and accurate alternative to conventional physical testing, with
substantial implications for railway safety and maintenance planning.

\bigskip

\textbf{Keywords:} Railway ballast; drop-weight impact loading;
\gls{RDSO} gradation; Fouling Index; 3D structured-light scanning; particle
morphology; under-ballast mats; ballast degradation.

\begin{finding}[title=Apparatus]
A purpose-built large-scale drop-weight impact apparatus
(\SI{300}{\milli\metre} mould, \SI{50}{\kilogram} hammer,
\SIrange{200}{450}{\milli\metre} adjustable drop, in-line
\SI{5}{\tonne} load cell) was designed, fabricated, and commissioned at
Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya. The two drop heights bracket the Indian
Railways field stress envelope at \SI{275}{\kilo\pascal} /
\SI{147}{\joule} and \SI{523}{\kilo\pascal} / \SI{221}{\joule}.
\hfill (Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology})
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[title=Experimental campaign]
A nine-configuration factorial test matrix crossing three pad conditions
(\gls{UR} / \gls{RUBM} / \gls{PUBM}) with two base conditions
(\gls{FB} / \gls{RB}) and two impact energies
(\SI{300}{\milli\metre} / \SI{450}{\milli\metre} drop) was executed; the
three \gls{FB}--\SI{450}{\milli\metre} cells were excluded by design.
\hfill (Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology})
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[title=Bulk fouling response]
The Indian Railway Fouling Index \FIIN{} spans a 6.4-fold range across
the nine configurations, from \SI{0.533}{\percent} at \testlabel{T1}
(\gls{PUBM}--\gls{FB}--300) to \SI{3.386}{\percent} at \testlabel{T7}
(\gls{UR}--\gls{RB}--450). \emph{Pad performance is energy- and
base-dependent}: \gls{PUBM} dominates at both extremes, but \gls{RUBM}
edges ahead on the rigid base at low energy.
\hfill (Chapter~\ref{chap:impact-results})
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[title=Two damage modes (Mode A / Mode B)]
A paired per-particle 3D morphological protocol on 45 tracer particles
revealed two distinct damage modes. \gls{ModeA} (diffuse corner
abrasion) is universal: every particle exhibits a small but
statistically significant sphericity rise
($\Delta\Psi = +0.0041$, $p < 10^{-6}$). \gls{ModeB} (catastrophic
fracture) is rare (6/45) and \emph{occurs only on rigid-base
configurations}.
\hfill (Chapter~\ref{chap:morph-results})
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[title=Settlement vs.\ fouling are non-interchangeable]
The test with the largest settlement (\testlabel{T5},
\SI{48.6}{\milli\metre}) is not the test with the largest fouling
(\testlabel{T7}). Settlement reflects rearrangement; fouling reflects
breakage. Both metrics are required for a complete bulk-level
characterisation.
\hfill (Chapter~\ref{chap:impact-results})
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[title=Output]
A utility-patent draft (\cref{chap:appB}); two journal manuscripts
under review; two accepted Springer conference papers; an open-source
Python pipeline (\texttt{ballast\_analyzer.py}) that ingests paired
pre/post \gls{STL} meshes and exports twenty-two morphological
descriptors per particle.
\hfill (Chapters~\ref{chap:conclusions} \& \ref{chap:appB})
\end{finding}
